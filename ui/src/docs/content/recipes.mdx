Recipe Folios keep ingredients, steps, and notes structured in Clepsydra while
remaining ordinary, portable Markdown files.

## Create and file a recipe

Create a page with kind `RECIPE`, or assign `RECIPE` to an existing page. A
recipe without a project is filed canonically under `recipes/`. Assigning the
kind can move the page to that folder, but it does not rewrite the page body.

The recipe title belongs to normal Clepsydra page metadata. Do not repeat it as
a heading or title line in the body. Clepsydra may remove an initial body line
only when it exactly matches the metadata title after trimming; arbitrary first
lines remain description text and never replace the metadata title.

## Recipe format

The canonical recipe body uses `##` section headings, `-` ingredient bullets,
and `1.` numbered steps:

```markdown
Clean, clear chicken broth with charred aromatics. Makes four bowls.

## Ingredients

- 200g flour

### For the sauce

- 2 tomatoes
- 1 clove garlic

## Steps

1. Make the dough.
   Rest it 30 minutes under a cloth.
2. Heat the oven to 240C.

### For the sauce

1. Blanch the tomatoes.
2. Blend.

## Notes

**Batch-friendly**: the broth freezes well.
```

Everything before `## Ingredients` is the description. Every ingredient and
step is an opaque string: Clepsydra preserves quantities, punctuation,
temperatures, units, emphasis, and wording. It does not parse measurements or
perform unit conversion. Content after `## Notes` remains Markdown.

The three section headings are matched case-insensitively and must use a
consistent heading level. The sections must appear once each, in the order
Ingredients, Steps, Notes. Empty sections are accepted when their headings are
present.

A `###` heading one level below a section opens a **component group** — a
sauce, a dough, an assembly. Items before the first group heading belong to the
section's **lead group**, the unnamed group every section has even when
nothing else does. Ingredients and Steps group independently: naming a group
under Ingredients does not create a matching group under Steps, so the
`### For the sauce` heading appears once in each section above. Step numbers
restart at 1 in each group. A named group is written even when it holds no
items; the lead group is written only when it holds some.

A step may carry indented continuation lines, kept verbatim as part of that
step, as `Rest it 30 minutes under a cloth.` is above. Ingredients stay
single-line — an indented line under an ingredient is not accepted.

Creating a page with kind `RECIPE` and no body writes the three empty headings
for you, so a new recipe opens structured and ready to fill in rather than in
the fallback below. An already-empty body is read the same way.

Clepsydra also reads a legacy form: bare `INGREDIENTS`, `STEPS`, and `NOTES`
markers with `•` bullets and no heading level.

```markdown
Clean, clear chicken broth with charred aromatics. Makes four bowls.

INGREDIENTS
• 1.5 kilograms whole chicken
• 2 large brown onions, halved

STEPS
1. Char the aromatics until blackened in patches.
2. Poach the chicken at a bare simmer.

NOTES
**Batch-friendly**: The broth freezes well.
```

A legacy body opens structured like any other, but it cannot carry component
groups — a heading anywhere inside it falls back to Markdown. It is rewritten
to the canonical heading form only when you save a structured edit; opening it,
or merely assigning `RECIPE`, does not touch the source.

## Structured editing

A supported recipe opens with dedicated read and edit views for its
description, ingredients, steps, and notes. Description and notes are edited
as Markdown.

Ingredients and Steps each edit as a textarea per group — one for the lead
group, then one more per named group — with one item per line, so a block of
ingredients or steps pasted from a website lands as separate items.
Any list marker on a pasted line — `-`, `*`, `+`, `•`, `1.`, `1)` — is stripped
on the way in; the textarea itself never shows markers or step numbers, so
typing never fights renumbering. In the Steps textarea, an indented line
continues the step above it rather than starting a new one.

**Add group** appends a new group with an empty name field for you to fill in.
**Remove group** deletes a group and moves its items into the group above, so
removing one never discards written text. Clearing a group's name has the same
effect once the body is written: an unnamed group is not a valid group, so its
items merge into the group above.

Saving from the structured editor always writes the canonical body described
above: `##` section headings, `-` ingredient bullets, sequential step numbers
that restart per group, normalized spacing and line endings, and one final
newline. A legacy recipe is rewritten to this form only after an explicit
structured edit and save.

## When Clepsydra falls back to Markdown

If Clepsydra cannot account for the complete body without losing content, it
shows a preservation notice and uses the generic Markdown editor instead of the
structured recipe editor. Examples include:

- a section heading that is missing, duplicated, or out of order
- inconsistent heading levels between the three sections
- a group heading at the wrong depth, or with no name
- a group heading inside a legacy-form body
- an ingredient or step line Clepsydra cannot parse as a list item
- a `[ ]` or `[x]` task-list marker leading an ingredient or step

That last one applies to the heading format only: the same text in the
legacy form (`1. [ ] Prepare broth.`) is kept as ordinary, opaque step text
rather than triggering the fallback.

The original Markdown remains authoritative in fallback mode. Editing and
saving through the generic editor preserves unsupported Markdown rather than
silently dropping or canonicalizing it. To enable structured editing, reshape
the body into one of the accepted formats above.
